# Aion, a time globe

**Turn the globe. Move the time. See what happened where.**

Aion is a free, non-commercial globe with a time slider. You choose a moment, from the last ice age to the present, and the map moves with you: ice caps and coastlines change, and the places where people built, worshipped, fought, traded and suffered light up as pins. Every pin opens a page with what is known about that place, **when**, and **how sure we are**.

The idea is simple and slightly stubborn: *the information about the past is scattered over thousands of books, databases and museum pages. Put it on one globe, in layers you can switch on and off, with filters that let you ask your own question, and make it honest about its evidence.*

## What you can do with it

- **Scrub through time.** A slider runs from about 150,000 years ago to today. A density curve shows how much of the record sits at each moment, and two strips show the ice-age cycles and the greening of the Sahara.
- **Watch the Earth change.** Ice, sea level and the green of the land follow the date you pick.
- **Switch layers on and off.** Satellite imagery and a road map, historical borders, nature parks, museums, large natural disasters, impact craters, cities and more.
- **Filter by evidence.** Every date on every pin carries a grade, and the globe colours it for you.
- **Open a pin.** A short description, the dates with their own sources, a photo with its credit and licence, coordinates, and a deeper page where one exists.
- **Listen instead of reading.** Pins can be read aloud.
- **Use it on a phone.** The layers drawer becomes a bottom sheet and the targets are finger-sized.

## The one rule that matters: honesty about evidence

Every date is graded, and the grade is visible:

| Grade | Colour | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Mainstream** | green | Peer-reviewed consensus |
| **Disputed** | amber | A real place with contested dating or interpretation. All sides are cited. |
| **Speculative** | purple | Hypothesis territory, clearly labelled |

Speculation is never deleted, and never dressed up as fact. And a place can be well established while its date is disputed (or the other way round), so the identification of a place and the dating of it are graded separately.

Read more: [Evidence and sources](docs/evidence-and-sources.md).

## What is on the globe today

Roughly 1,500 archaeological and historical sites, several hundred natural disasters and impact structures, about a hundred museums, parks, cities, climate periods, and a political map that changes with the date. Planned layers include ancient royal roads, pilgrimage routes and a careful layer on what ancient DNA says about migration. Faith places and sacred sites, and submerged landscapes such as the North Sea basin, are being added and are not yet on the live globe. No dates are promised. See [Layers and data](docs/layers-and-data.md).

## Who is behind it, and why

Aion is a private project of one person, built in a home lab with the help of a team of AI agents who gather, check and cross-review the entries. There is no company, no advertising network and no business model. Nobody sells anything here and nobody is going to. The point is to make the human record of the past easier to explore for anybody who is curious.

The agents are not the authority. Sources are. Every entry is checked against the pages it cites, and anything that cannot be verified is removed rather than softened. How that works day to day is described in [Behind the scenes](docs/behind-the-scenes.md).

## We are looking for people

You do not need to be an expert. Here is how you can help:

- **Know the past?** Tell us where a date or a source is wrong, or which place is missing.
- **Know where good data lives?** Open datasets, excavation reports, museum collections, university projects: tell us where to look.
- **Have an idea for a layer?** Trade routes, language families, plagues, megaliths, shipwrecks, ancient observatories: tell us what you would want to explore.
- **Build things?** Maps, accessibility, translations, data cleaning and tests are all welcome topics.
- **Teach or write?** Tell us what a globe like this would need to be useful in a classroom.

The easiest way is the **[contact form](https://aion.eanna.house/site/contact.html)**. Details on what makes a good suggestion are in [Contributing](docs/contributing.md).

## A note on the sources

The globe stands on other people's work: NASA, the Copernicus programme, the US Geological Survey, NOAA, OpenStreetMap and its contributors, Wikipedia contributors, and many open projects and institutions. Each layer credits its source on the About page and in the app itself. If we have misread a licence or left out a credit, please say so and we will fix it.
