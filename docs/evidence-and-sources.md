# Evidence and sources

Aion treats evidence as the main feature, not a footnote. This page explains how entries are graded, where facts come from, and what is refused.

## Grades

Every **date** on a pin has its own grade, source and badge.

- **Mainstream** (green): peer-reviewed consensus.
- **Disputed** (amber): a real place with a contested interpretation or dating, with all sides cited.
- **Speculative** (purple): hypothesis territory, clearly labelled as such.

Two things are graded separately:

1. **The date**: when did this happen, or when was this built?
2. **The identification**: is this the place a text or tradition names? (This matters for places such as ancient cities and sites named in old texts.)

A place can be certain while its date is disputed, and the other way round. Mixing the two gives wrong impressions both ways.

## One rule above all: real and sourced

- Every entry must be real and traceable to a source that can be opened.
- A sentence that cannot be verified is removed, not softened.
- When several independent sources support a fact, that makes "mainstream" firmer.
- When sources disagree, the entry says so and cites each side.
- Speculative material is kept, labelled, and never presented as fact.

## Where facts come from, in order

When we add or touch a pin, we look for the fact in this order and use the highest tier that has it:

1. **Authoritative and primary.** UNESCO World Heritage, peer-reviewed publications, national heritage and archaeology institutes, and encyclopaedias with editorial verification.
2. **Reliable secondary.** Major museums and institutes, the World History Encyclopedia, and Wikipedia, used as a starting point whose own sources we follow and cite instead of the page itself.
3. **Wider web, as a last resort.** Only when the first two tiers do not cover the fact, graded conservatively, with the uncertainty stated.

What we refuse as a source of fact: content farms, AI-generated sites, blogs without sources, and marketplace or tourism promotion.

## Neutrality

Where a place matters to a tradition, the text says whose claim it is ("according to ...") and does not rank traditions against each other. The same two-source rule and the same grade rule apply to every tradition and every period.

## What a pin shows

In this order: a short name; audio; a photo with credit and licence; a short description; the dates, each with its grade and a clickable source; coordinates; a deeper page where one exists; one source list at the bottom.

A pin always has at least one dated event and a description. There are no empty pins.

## Photos

Only images with a clear licence. The credit and the licence are shown next to the photo. If a licence cannot be read on the original file page, the pin gets no photo, with the reason and the date noted, instead of a risky one.

## Checking

Rules like these only work if something enforces them. A data checker runs on every change and refuses entries that break the rules: missing sources, unfinished sentences, names that are too long, tags that are not in the closed vocabulary, and more. Every change is also reviewed by a second reviewer who re-checks the cited pages, not only the summary.
