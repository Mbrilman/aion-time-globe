# Layers and data

## What is on the globe

Approximate counts at the time of writing:

| Layer | What it shows | Roughly |
|---|---|---|
| Sites | Archaeological and historical places, each with dated events and a grade | 1,500 |
| Natural disasters | Eruptions, earthquakes, floods and other large events | 270 |
| Impact structures | Meteorite craters and suspected impacts | 200 |
| Museums | Collections you can visit | 110 |
| Cities | Major cities through time | 50 |
| Nature parks | Outlines and short descriptions | 47 |
| Countries | Present-day outlines for orientation | 200 |
| Large igneous provinces | Enormous volcanic episodes | 17 |
| Historical borders | Political maps for chosen dates | a changing map |
| Climate | Ice-age cycles, Sahara greening, sea level | strips and a readout |
| Satellite and terrain | Imagery and hillshade | base layers |
| Road map | A modern road map from open data | base layer |

Some entries are intentionally marked as speculative (for example, objects whose age or origin is debated). They are shown and labelled, never hidden.

## Filters

- **Time**: a slider and a window around it (how near a date has to be to count as in focus).
- **Evidence grade**: show or hide mainstream, disputed and speculative dates.
- **Tags** (in development): a closed vocabulary of tags lets you ask questions such as "show only places of worship that are identified with certainty". A tag never claims more than the entry's own text.

## Being added, and planned

Being added, not yet on the live globe:

- **Faith places and sacred sites**, grouped by what the place is (temple, church, mosque, synagogue, shrine and so on), whose it is, how sure its identification is, and which text names it. Neutral wording for every tradition is a requirement, not a nicety.
- **Submerged landscapes**, such as the lost land and rivers of the North Sea basin.

Planned, no dates promised:

- **Royal roads and courier systems** of the ancient world, built from the primary descriptions where possible.
- **Pilgrimage routes** as lines.
- **Ancient DNA finds** as a small, careful layer: population samples in aggregate, and a short list of famous published single finds only when they are old, published once and free of any recorded objection from descendant communities.

## Where pins come from

Public data and public scholarship: heritage lists, excavation reports, peer-reviewed papers, museum collections and open datasets. If you know a source we do not use, that is exactly the kind of tip we want ([Contributing](contributing.md)).
